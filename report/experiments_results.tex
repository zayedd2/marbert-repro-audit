\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:results}

All results rest on 36 fine-tuning runs (two models, three datasets, \rsNSeeds{} seeds and two
protocols), together with six AJGT check runs on the authors' split and six single-seed follow-up runs.
Every run saved its test predictions, and its dev predictions where a dev set existed, for all 25 epochs.
One script in our public repository\footnote{\url{https://github.com/zayedd2/marbert-repro-audit}} rebuilds
every table, figure and number in this section from those files, after re-scoring each saved prediction
set against the logged metrics. This section reports what we observed;
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} interprets it.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:results-setup}

All runs use the settings in Section~\ref{sec:method} on a single \rsGPU{} and took \rsGPUHours{} GPU-hours
in total, \rsGPUHoursAstd{} of them for ASTD. The test sets hold \rsNTestArSarc{} (ArSarcasm-Sent),
\rsNTestAstd{} (ASTD) and \rsNTestAjgt{} (AJGT) tweets. Historical runs train on the full training sets
(\rsNTrainHistArSarc{}, \rsNTrainHistAstd{} and \rsNTrainHistAjgt{} tweets) and keep the epoch with the best
test score under the published metric, as [1] did. Independent runs hold out \rsNDevArSarc{},
\rsNDevAstd{} and \rsNDevAjgt{} dev tweets and keep the epoch with the best dev score under the same metric,
so the test set plays no part in selection. Ties go to the earlier epoch, and all metrics of a run are read
at its selected checkpoint.

We report training randomness as the mean and standard deviation (SD) over seeds, and test-sample
randomness as a 95\% paired bootstrap interval over test tweets (\rsBootB{} resamples), always separately.
Following our proposal, a published score counts as reproduced if our mean is within \rsGapThreshold{}
points of it or the published score lies inside our seed range, and MARBERT still outscores AraBERT on that
dataset.

Under the historical protocol, \rsNReproduced{} of the \rsNTargets{} published scores meet the reproduction

MARBERT reaches \rsHistScoreArSarcMarbert{} F1-PN on ArSarcasm-Sent against \rsPubArSarcMarbert{} in [1]

\rsGapAstdArabert{} on ASTD, MARBERT by \rsGapAstdMarbert{} on ASTD, and AraBERT by \rsGapAjgtArabert{}

On the authors' own AJGT split, both scores are reproduced: MARBERT reaches \rsHistScoreAjgtUbcMarbert{}
(gap \rsGapAjgtUbcMarbert{}) and AraBERT \rsHistScoreAjgtUbcArabert{} (gap \rsGapAjgtUbcArabert{}), with
both published values inside the seed range.

The ranking holds on all \rsNRankHoldHist{} datasets, and MARBERT's lowest seed beats AraBERT's highest on
each. The lead is smaller than in [1] on ArSarcasm-Sent (\rsMarginHistArSarcHm{} against 8.00 points) and
ASTD (\rsMarginHistAstdHm{} against 6.00), and larger on AJGT (\rsMarginHistAjgtHm{} against 3.89). Six
follow-up runs on ArSarcasm-Sent (Appendix, Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity}) change the AraBERT release, its
preprocessing, the learning-rate schedule or the learning rate. None moves F1-PN by more than 1.12 points,
and AraBERT stays at least 4.10 points above its published 63.50.

\subsection{RQ4: Metric choice}
\label{sec:results-rq4}

The ranking never changes with the metric: MARBERT leads under F1-PN, macro-F1 and accuracy on every
dataset and under both protocols (Table~\ref{tab:margins}). The size of the lead does change. On
ArSarcasm-Sent the independent lead is \rsMarginIndArSarcHm{} points in F1-PN and \rsMarginIndArSarcMf{} in
macro-F1.

ASTD shows the largest difference between metrics. MARBERT scores \rsIndHmAstdMarbert{} in F1-PN but
\rsIndMfAstdMarbert{} in macro-F1, because the neutral (mixed) class, which F1-PN leaves out, reaches only
\rsPcAstdMarbertNeutral{} F1 for MARBERT and \rsPcAstdArabertNeutral{} for AraBERT (Appendix,
Table~\ref{tab:perclass}). Under the independent protocol the ASTD lead shrinks from \rsMarginIndAstdHm{}
points in F1-PN to \rsMarginIndAstdMf{} in macro-F1, whereas under the historical protocol it grows from
\rsMarginHistAstdHm{} to \rsMarginHistAstdMf{}. On AJGT, with
two balanced classes, accuracy and macro-F1 give the same lead (\rsMarginIndAjgtHm{} and
\rsMarginIndAjgtMf{}).

\subsection{Error analysis}
\label{sec:results-errors}

Section~\ref{sec:errors} breaks the errors down by dialect and sarcasm; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:subgroups}
adds paired bootstrap intervals to those subgroup results. Under the independent protocol, MARBERT leads on
non-sarcastic tweets (\rsSubIndNonSarcMf{} macro-F1, CI \rsSubIndNonSarcMfCI{}) but not on the
\rsSubNSarc{} sarcastic ones (\rsSubIndSarcMf{}, CI \rsSubIndSarcMfCI{}). The intervals for the small Gulf
(\rsSubNGulf{} tweets, \rsSubIndGulfMfCI{}) and Levantine groups (\rsSubNLev{} tweets, \rsSubIndLevMfCI{})
are wide.
